Three rules

Glass is for the layer that floats.

Bars, capsules, controls over media and sheets at partial height are chrome, and only chrome is glass. Cards, tables, charts and numbers stay opaque, and glass never sits on glass — it cannot sample what another lens has already bent.

One edge per scroll view.

The edge effect separates floating controls from the content moving under them. It is not decoration: it appears only once something is actually underneath. Soft dissolves the content; hard is a near-opaque band for dense bars and text outside glass. Scroll either pane.

A run starts once on a project and works the tasks it finds there, one row at a time. The bar above stays out of the way until the words reach it.

Soft, for glass controls

The glass groups in this bar hold every label they need, so the edge only has to soften what passes beneath. Watch the paragraph blur and fade as it slides under the back button.

Over dark content the same edge becomes a subtle dimming instead of a white fade, because the glass above it has gone dark too.

There is one edge per scroll view and per side. A second one in the same container is refused, with a warning in the console.

Scroll back to the top and the edge goes: with nothing underneath, there is nothing to separate.

That is the whole component: a zero-height anchor at the top of the scroller, two layers hanging from it, and one attribute that says which style it is.

data-edge-style="soft" — glass controls, no text outside them.

Recordings keep a short flight record of every run: the screens it saw, the steps it took and the rows it wrote.

Hard, for dense bars

This bar has a title that sits outside any glass. Soft would let the paragraphs read through the title; the hard edge is a linear, nearly opaque band with a hairline, like a hard-edged native bar.

Automatic picks hard for you when the region holds a pinned header or a dense toolbar, and soft everywhere else.

In a split view each pane may have its own edge. Keep their heights equal so the two bars line up.

Vertical bars, like the side strip on a large canvas, get no edge at all by default.

Under increased contrast the soft edge turns solid, and in forced colours it becomes a plain band of the canvas colour.

The chrome API at a glance.
