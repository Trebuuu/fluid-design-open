The role model

A job, not a hex code.

A page never names a colour. It names a job — the page ground, the secondary label, the primary fill — and the palette answers for the tone, the brand and the reader’s contrast setting. One accent goes in and every role comes out, each held to a measured ratio against the hardest ground it can land on.

          Fills

          Alphas, so they work anywhere.

          A control fill has to sit on a card, on a band and on glass, so the four fills are tinted alphas, not greys. The ink inside them is its own role: --label-2-on-fill is the pill ink, because the page’s secondary grey has no headroom left once a fill is under it.

          On art

          Ink for the art.

          --on-art-1 and --on-art-2 are near-whites tinted toward the brand, fitted against art no lighter than OKLCH L 0.40 under the copy. The painter’s palettes run brighter than that, so the art is held down where words sit. Both cards are measured from their own pixels.

          System colours.

          Reference values: light, dark, and both increased-contrast steps, shown as the four dots. Blue is #0088ff now, not #007aff. They are fills for glyphs and marks, not held to a text ratio — text uses the labels and the accent.

          Data

          Six hues that survive colour blindness.

          The default series is a six-hue palette, ordered so neighbours stay apart under red and green colour blindness. Red and pink are left out — red means stop.

          Increased contrast is a step, not a target.

          Standard

          Increased contrast

          When a reader asks for more contrast, every ink moves one fixed step toward the ink and every fill thickens. A step keeps the four label levels apart; refitting everything to one higher target collapses --label-2 and --label-3 into the same grey. The right panel is data-contrast="more" on a wrapper — the same switch prefers-contrast: more throws.

          Bring your own colour.

          Pick any accent. The engine fits every role in both tones, checks each against its hardest ground and tells you what fails. Pin the button to your exact colour and watch the report catch it.
