Composition

One idea per viewport.

A product page is a column of bands in a steady rhythm: white for the story, grey for galleries and utilities, black for the stage. Each band says one thing, with one picture, and the next band starts when that thing is finished.

This page…

This page, band by band, measured live: bar height is the band’s height, the figure its top and bottom padding. The map fades up on a keyframe written straight into its markup.

Story bands are white with 128 px above and below. Grey bands take 144, and neighbours merge into one sheet; two white bands keep both paddings. A band may fade into the next instead of cutting.

Pictures come from three places only: ART3 art, DEVICE frames with live screens, and compositions of our own components and charts. Never third-party imagery, product photographs, or trade dress.

Glass stays chrome here too: the hero platter, the control bar, pose chips and paddles. Cards, tiles, sheets at full height and every number are opaque.

Motion follows scroll, calmly. Content is written in its final state and the choreography only brings it there; reduced motion and the base experience show everything, in place.

Local only

It runs on your desk.

Orbit runs on the computer your runners are plugged into, and you watch every run from there. The recorder’s export is a zip you choose to send, with every name and message stripped first.

Recorder

The phone tells you what happened.

Every run keeps a short flight record: the screens it saw, the steps it took and the rows it wrote. When something goes wrong on a runner you cannot reach, the export carries the evidence, not the people behind it.

Queues

Empty, or never there?

A run that reaches a review list and sweeps it without finding anything new writes that queue off. A run that timed out — the list did not open, or the screen was not the one expected — changes nothing: the queue is held for fifteen minutes and tried again. Of the last 38 runs, 14 came back empty and 1 timed out.

Ledger

The limit counts rows.

Every action the runner completes writes its own row: one build, one test, one deploy. A run is sized in tasks, and a build can touch several files per task, so the daily limit of 2,160 counts rows, never tasks and never what was asked. Today that is 1,222 completed of 1,904 asked.
